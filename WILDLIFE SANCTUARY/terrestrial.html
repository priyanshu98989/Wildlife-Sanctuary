Terrestrial Wildlife Sanctuary

A majestic Bengal tiger prowls silently through the dense forest, its black stripes blending with the filtered sunlight and shadows. This powerful predator relies on stealth and strength, stalking prey like deer and wild boar. Tigers are often found near water sources and are most active during dawn and dusk.

Resting gracefully on a tree branch, the leopard keeps watch from above. With strong limbs and a long tail for balance, it often drags its kill up into trees to avoid scavengers. Stealthy, agile, and solitary, the leopard is one of the most elusive hunters of the forest.

A herd of elephants marches through the forest path, led by a matriarch. These social animals travel in female-led groups and feed on grasses, fruits, and tree bark. Their daily movement helps shape the forest, clearing paths and dispersing seeds as they go.

A group of deer grazes quietly at the forest edge, alert to any sounds. They feed on grasses and foliage, and their cautious movements are a defense mechanism against predators. Often seen during the early morning or evening, deer are a vital part of the woodland food chain.

The forest is home to a wide variety of mammals—large and small. From jungle cats and mongooses to wild boars and langurs, these animals maintain the ecosystem's balance. Their roles include dispersing seeds, preying on insects or small animals, and serving as indicators of forest health.

This type of sanctuary protects land-based species such as tigers, elephants, deer and other wildlife in forests, grasslands, and woodlands.
