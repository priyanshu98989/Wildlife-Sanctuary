Bird Sanctuary

A graceful flock of flamingos wades through shallow saline waters, their pink‑tinged feathers glowing at sunrise. These birds filter‑feed by holding their uniquely shaped bills upside‑down, sifting brine shrimp, algae, and crustaceans through lamellae rooted in their tongue and bill interior—carotenoids from their diet give flamingos their vivid colour. Breeding colonies may number in the thousands, engaging in synchronized courtship displays and relying on dense flocks to guard against predators. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

A pair of cranes stands poised in shallow marshland, necks extended and long legs partially submerged. These birds feed on a mix of invertebrates, fish, amphibians, and tubers found along watery edges. In South Asia a resident species like the Sarus crane—an elegant, large grey bird with a crimson head—often nests in flooded rice paddies or wetlands, performs unison calling and courtship dances, and maintains long-term monogamous pair‑bonds within defended territories. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

A sweeping V‑formation of migratory waterbirds—including bar‑headed geese, pelicans, and waders—descends upon the wetland during winter. These birds travel thousands of kilometres from frozen breeding grounds in Siberia and Central Asia to winter sites like Pong Lake, where tens of thousands gather across 48 species. Their arrival on mudflats and shorelines forms vital seasonal pulses of energy and nutrients in the ecosystem. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

A grey heron wades amid marsh grasses, its sleek form and pointed bill held in ready stillness. As an apex ambush hunter, it stands motionless before striking fish, amphibians, insects, and small mammals with a lightning jab. Herons forage alone or in small groups along marshes, estuaries, or reed beds, and their feeding behaviour serves as a key indicator of wetland health and water quality. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

A vibrant scene of tropical devotees: kingfishers shimmer in cobalt and rufous as they dive for fish, bee‑eaters flash emerald and chestnut while snatching insects mid‑air, and rose‑ringed parakeets chatter in neon green flocks. These colourful birds play crucial roles in their ecosystems—pollinating flowers, natural pest control, and dispersing seeds—each evolved with specialized colouration, feathers, or beaks suited to its ecological niche. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
