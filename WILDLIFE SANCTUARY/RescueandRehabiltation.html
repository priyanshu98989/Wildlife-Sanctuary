Rescue & Rehabilitation Sanctuary

Wildlife rescuers bring in an injured wild animal, stabilizing it amid controlled stress. The team performs an initial field triage—covering wounds, immobilizing limbs, and preparing the animal for safe transport to a licensed rehabilitator. This emergency response jump‑starts the clinical rehabilitation journey.

A veterinary professional administers specialized medical care in a wildlife treatment unit—cleaning wounds, administering IV fluids, pain control, and conducting diagnostic exams. The setting is a purpose‑built wildlife hospital with trained staff handling species‑specific need.

A young orphaned creature—perhaps a bird or mammal—receives nutritional support in a carefully controlled environment. Caregivers provide species‑appropriate formula, warmth, and minimal human interaction to avoid imprinting. If possible, juveniles are raised with conspecifics so they can learn natural behaviours before release.

In its recovery enclosure, the animal regains mobility and survivor instincts: naturalistic furnishings—perches, scrub, or similar substrates—encourage climbing, perching, or foraging. Human access is limited to reduce habituation, following best practices for pre‑release conditioning.

The rehabilitated animal is gently released into its native habitat—following thorough health checks and site evaluation. Depending on species and circumstances, a soft‑release approach allows acclimatization in on‑site holding pens, followed by monitored freedom using radio tags or visual surveys.
